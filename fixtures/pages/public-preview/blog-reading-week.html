Field notes from a quiet reading week

How a little Spanish helped me stay with a long essay

I usually read technical essays in the evening, when my energy is low and the browser is full of small distractions. This week I tried a slower routine: one article, one notebook, and a clear plan to stop after twenty minutes.

The first surprise was that small help can be enough. I did not need to translate the whole page, and I did not want a full lesson. I only wanted a few familiar words to light up the path while I kept reading.

The best moment came when I saw an old phrase in a new paragraph and understood it before opening help.

By the way, the phrase did not interrupt the essay. It appeared like a small marker, and as soon as I clicked it, the explanation was short enough that I could return to the sentence.

I still need to watch my pace. Too many highlights can make a good essay feel like homework, and a right answer is not the goal during a normal reading session. The goal is to stay with the page.

At home I now leave the extension on for blogs, documentation, and local news. I pause it for forms, account pages, and anything private, because those pages are not part of my reading practice.
